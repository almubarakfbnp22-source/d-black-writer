The Invitation

She asked me, if death came to me and looking into my eyes, what I would say. I would say, What took you so long? I've been sending invitations all this while, but you refused to show up. If I die, just box me up. I don't need a headstone, no name, no design. — D Black Writer

Beyond the First Glance

She came to the bank not knowing she would leave with a poem written about her. Just another customer, another face passing through the doors, another ordinary day pretending to be ordinary. But then I saw her. A woman whose darkness did not ask for light, because she carried her own kind of glow. — D Black Writer
Work & Humanity

Behind the Name Tag

They see the smile... but never the pressure. The targets that steal our sleep. The customers we calm. The late nights we endure. Every morning, we wear our name tags—but quietly leave pieces of ourselves at the branch door. We stand behind the same counter, wear the same colours, and serve the same customers. Yet when the day is done, some are celebrated while others remain invisible. Experience bows to status, loyalty waits behind titles, and the hands that built the bridge are too often told they cannot cross it. Still, we return. Not because the burden is light, but because our families are waiting, our dreams refuse to die, and hope is the only salary no one can take from us. We are the silent strength behind every balanced account, every resolved complaint, every target achieved. So when you walk into a bank, remember this: behind every transaction is a human being carrying battles you'll never see. We are more than support. We are more than job titles. We are the voices of thousands whose sacrifices keep the doors open, still waiting for the day hard work is measured not by labels, but by worth. — D Black Writer
Nigeria & Hope

Nigeria Cry But Hope Remain

Dear Nigeria... They call you the Giant of Africa, yet your children sleep with one eye open. A mother kisses her child goodbye for school, but spends the rest of the day praying that goodbye is not forever. The classroom has become a place of fear, and innocent voices have been replaced with cries that no child should ever know. Our streets are filled with hardworking people, yet empty pockets grow heavier every day. The farmer fears the journey to his own farm, the trader counts losses instead of profits, and the youth carry dreams bigger than their opportunities. Still, we wake each morning believing tomorrow must be kinder than today. To the families whose children and teachers were taken by violence, your tears are our tears. To the soldiers standing between danger and our homes, we salute your courage. To every Nigerian who refuses to surrender hope despite hardship, you remind us that strength is not the absence of pain—it is choosing to rise in the middle of it. Nigeria, you are bleeding, but you are not defeated. One day, the sound of children's laughter will drown out the echoes of gunfire. One day, justice will silence fear, peace will replace sorrow, and hope will no longer be a prayer but a reality. Until then, we will keep believing, because no darkness has ever defeated the promise of a new dawn. — D Black Writer

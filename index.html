Featured poem

The Invitation

She asked me, if death came to me and looking into my eyes, what I would say. I would say, What took you so long? I've been sending invitations all this while, but you refused to show up. If I die, just box me up. I don't need a headstone, no name, no design. Don’t waste your time trying to make me memorable. I am a piece of shit that will be forgotten anyway. Maybe that is all I was ever meant to be— a name that fades, a face that becomes a memory, a memory that eventually disappears. This life is a dark place, and I don't like the dark. I don't want to remain here. I don't want to spend forever in this forsaken place. To the one who asks why I don't love them, how could I, if I don't even love myself? How can I give you a love I've never known how to give myself? How can I teach my heart to hold you when it has never learned how to hold me? Perhaps that is the cruelest part— you loved me while I was still learning how to survive myself. And if you ever wonder where I went, I was only looking for a place where the darkness couldn't find me. — D Black Writer

Beyond the First Glance

She came to the bank not knowing she would leave with a poem written about her. Just another customer, another face passing through the doors, another ordinary day pretending to be ordinary. But then I saw her. A woman whose darkness did not ask for light, because she carried her own kind of glow. — DHer skin held the quiet of midnight, deep, warm, like a secret the moon was never allowed to keep. She was beautiful— but beauty was only the first thing I noticed. Because somewhere between “Hello” and “How are you?” between one conversation and another, I began to discover that her beauty had a deeper meaning. She was thoughtful— the kind of thoughtful that makes you realize some people don't just listen to words, they listen to the person saying them. She was brilliant. Smart. A mind that could stand beside her beauty and somehow make it even more beautiful. And beneath it all, I found respect. I found kindness. I found a woman who knew how to carry herself without needing to announce her worth. You think you're attracted to her face, until you discover her mind. You think it's her smile, until you discover her heart. You think it's the beauty that keeps drawing you closer, until you realize it is the person herself. And if God ever asks me what I discovered that day, I might tell Him: I discovered that beauty is not always something you see. Sometimes, it is something you discover. Something you hear in a person's words, feel in their respect, notice in their kindness, and slowly begin to admire without even realizing it. She came as a customer. But she left an impression. And now I understand why the world has spent centuries searching for enough words to describe a woman. Because perhaps there were never enough words to begin with. Women are among the most beautiful things God has ever created. - Black Writer
Work & Humanity

Behind the Name Tag

They see the smile... but never the pressure. The targets that steal our sleep. The customers we calm. The late nights we endure. Every morning, we wear our name tags—but quietly leave pieces of ourselves at the branch door. We stand behind the same counter, wear the same colours, and serve the same customers. Yet when the day is done, some are celebrated while others remain invisible. Experience bows to status, loyalty waits behind titles, and the hands that built the bridge are too often told they cannot cross it. Still, we return. Not because the burden is light, but because our families are waiting, our dreams refuse to die, and hope is the only salary no one can take from us. We are the silent strength behind every balanced account, every resolved complaint, every target achieved. So when you walk into a bank, remember this: behind every transaction is a human being carrying battles you'll never see. We are more than support. We are more than job titles. We are the voices of thousands whose sacrifices keep the doors open, still waiting for the day hard work is measured not by labels, but by worth. — D Black Writer
Nigeria & Hope

Nigeria Cry But Hope Remain

Dear Nigeria... They call you the Giant of Africa, yet your children sleep with one eye open. A mother kisses her child goodbye for school, but spends the rest of the day praying that goodbye is not forever. The classroom has become a place of fear, and innocent voices have been replaced with cries that no child should ever know. Our streets are filled with hardworking people, yet empty pockets grow heavier every day. The farmer fears the journey to his own farm, the trader counts losses instead of profits, and the youth carry dreams bigger than their opportunities. Still, we wake each morning believing tomorrow must be kinder than today. To the families whose children and teachers were taken by violence, your tears are our tears. To the soldiers standing between danger and our homes, we salute your courage. To every Nigerian who refuses to surrender hope despite hardship, you remind us that strength is not the absence of pain—it is choosing to rise in the middle of it. Nigeria, you are bleeding, but you are not defeated. One day, the sound of children's laughter will drown out the echoes of gunfire. One day, justice will silence fear, peace will replace sorrow, and hope will no longer be a prayer but a reality. Until then, we will keep believing, because no darkness has ever defeated the promise of a new dawn. — D Black Writer
